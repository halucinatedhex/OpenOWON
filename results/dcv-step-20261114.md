# DC volts autorange step time, 2026-11-14 (T-DCV / T-RANGE)

`xdm1241-ui` at RATE F, autorange. Bench supply preset to 25 V, its output
switched on and off by hand. MEAS? and RANGE? polled ~35 times a second
(`dcv-step-20261114.tsv`: seconds, MEAS? reply, RANGE? reply; 9.91E+37 is
overload or no reading yet).

**0 -> 25 V.** Ranging goes as designed: 50 mV overloads, the app jumps
to 1000 V, then straight to 50 V (two changes; stock steps one range at a
time). Each change shows overload for 0.7-0.9 s before a reading:

| Step | first change | 24.999 V shown | time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 15.989 s | 17.708 s | 1.72 s |
| 2 | 23.635 s | 25.123 s | 1.49 s |
| 3 | 42.139 s | 43.611 s | 1.47 s |

Includes the supply's own rise (the first sample, 0.04 V, is mid-rise).
**Fails T-RANGE's 600 ms** (the simulator predicts 252 ms); stock is
~1.75 s per range change, so this is still ~2x faster than stock from 50 mV.

**Why.** At RATE F a conversion takes ~205 ms (steady-state update gap,
29 updates in 8 s). After each range change the app drops ACQ_DISCARD = 2
conversions and uses the third: 3 x 205 + 20 ms settle = ~0.64 s a change.
The simulator's conversions are faster than the chip's. Options (B10d/B11):
a faster OSR while hunting; fewer discards on the 1000 V hop, whose only
job is to choose the range.

**25 -> 0 V** is not usable: with only the meter's 10 MOhm as a load the
supply's output decays over ~2 s, and the meter ranges down chasing it,
one range at a time (by design: down-ranging steps). Repeat with a load
(e.g. 1 kOhm across the supply) for a clean falling edge.

## After the fixes, same day

**Fast hunt only** (eb65876, `dcv-step-20261114-fasthunt.tsv`): 0.77,
0.74, 0.78 s. Each range change still took ~0.35 s: the configure write
(25 registers) took ~80 ms on the bus at stock's 192 us half-bit, and the
hunt waited behind ~150 ms redraws.

**Fast hunt + 4 us bus + polling between bands while hunting** (7fb8037,
`dcv-step-20261114-fastbus.tsv`, 1 kOhm load across the supply). Main
loop ~2000 steps/s (was ~52). From the first sign of the step to 24.99 V
shown: 0.36, 0.36, 0.36, 0.37, 0.36 s; one 0.62 s where the logger caught
the supply mid-rise. The simulator predicts 362 ms. Add up to one
full-resolution conversion (~0.25 s) for the meter to notice the step on
50 mV: **0.4-0.6 s step to display, against 1.5 s before; T-RANGE's
600 ms is met, just, at worst.**

Falling edges (25 V -> 0 through 1 kOhm and the supply's own capacitance)
take ~1 s to decay, so they still time the supply, not the meter.
